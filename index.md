## Current Readings 

Here are a few latest books that I am reading/just finished. I am happy to receive book recommendations from anyone!
- **Remains of the Day (Kazuo Ishiguru):** A butler obsessed with his work reflects on his time serving Lord Darlington during the 1920s and 30s. The narrative style is eerily peculiar and quite interesting in my opinion. I actually liked this book by Ishiguro more than Never Let Me Go.
- **Origins of Political Order (Francis Fukuyama):** This is one of the quintessential non-fictions I have read. A deep historical analysis of political institutions: tribalism, chiefdom, role of ideas, religion etc. What impressed me most about the book is the author's cross civilizational analysis of developmental history of states in China, India, Europe and the Islamic world, which is extremely difficult to put together in one cover. This book *educated* me on history in a profound way. 
- **Political Order and Political Decay: From the Industrial Revolution to the Globalization of Democracy (Francis Fukuyama):** This is the second volume of the above book. I found it super interesting because it dives deep into the history of political institutions of the major geographic regions and explores phenomenon like government corruption, clientalistic politics and government efficiency. I learned a great deal about African and South American political history and post colonial legacies, which I knew very little about. If you have read the first volume, a few chapters would probably feel repetitive; but I still enjoyed the book. 
- **Postwar: A History of Europe Since 1945 (Tony Judt):** I never thought I would enjoy a history book *this* much. Totally recommend it for anyone interested in understanding present Europe better.
